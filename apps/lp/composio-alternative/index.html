The open-source, self-hosted Composio alternative

Composio is a solid managed platform. If you would rather keep OAuth tokens on your own server, pay nothing per seat and share memory across agents, runelay is the shortcut.

When runelay is the better fit

  • Your own agents. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Grok sharing the same accounts, skills and memory.
  • Data that cannot leave. Tokens and audit log stay on infrastructure you control.
  • No budget line. No seats to count and no call limits.
  • Low context cost. 1,045 tokens at connect for 125 tools. See the benchmark.

When Composio is the better fit

Pick Composio if you want a large catalog of integrations managed for you, you are building a product that authenticates your own end users, or you do not want to run anything at all. Different jobs, both good tools.

Switching takes a few minutes

  1. 1

    Install runelay

    On your computer or on any server with Docker.

    $curl -fsSL runelay.com/install | sh
  2. 2

    Import what your agents already have

    Accounts they were logged into arrive connected, the rest go into one reconnect queue.

    $runelay migrate
  3. 3

    Keep a Composio endpoint during the move

    runelay accepts any remote MCP URL, so a Composio endpoint can sit behind it until you replace it.

Questions

Is runelay really free?

Yes. It is MIT licensed, self-hosted and has no paid tier, seat price or call limit.

Can I self-host Composio instead?

Self-hosting Composio is an Enterprise arrangement. runelay is built to be self-hosted from day one: one desktop app or one Docker container with SQLite.

Does runelay have as many integrations?

No. It ships around 25 curated MCP servers with one-click OAuth, and adds anything else through a remote MCP, a local MCP or an OpenAPI spec.

Where are my OAuth tokens stored?

On your server only, encrypted with AES-256-GCM using your RUNELAY_SECRET.
